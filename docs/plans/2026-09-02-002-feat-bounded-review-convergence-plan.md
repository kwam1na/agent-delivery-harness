# feat: Bounded review convergence — declared round bound, verification rounds, severity-gated blocking

**Target repos:** `agent-skills` (workflow text, corpus test, release), `agent-delivery-harness` (installation, policy lens references, scripts, guidance), `athena` (installation, delivery overlay). Paths are relative to the repository named in each unit.

**Plan finish line.** Both adopters run the workflow release that bounds the review loop; the harness policy references the shipped reviewer charters by identity and its scripts resolve them from the installed archive; and the run-event ticket whose review loop did not converge (V26-1548) is re-run from its preserved pre-review candidate through the installed workflow and ends inside the bound after at least one verification round: either every lens aligned and a pull request open, or `partial` with `review.loop-bound-reached` naming the open P0 and P1 findings or the unreviewed change. A re-run that aligns in the assessment round alone is reported `partial` for the operator and does not finish the plan. Either way the one bound in effect, the total round count, and a disposition for every finding are on the ticket and in the round evidence, and the delivery obtained no round beyond the bound.

## Summary

On 2026-09-02 a delivery of the run-event contract (V26-1548) implemented its unit test-first with the gate green inside the first hour, then spent six more hours in seventeen review rounds without converging. The correctness lens was aligned for ten of those rounds. The testing-policy lens requested changes in every one, returning a fresh set of surviving mutants each time because the executor asked it, each round, to mount a new campaign where earlier rounds had not looked. The operator stopped the run and judged the cause a defect in the review workflow, not in the run.

Three omissions compounded. The workflow never told the executor to declare the round bound its own reducer requires, so the loop ran unbounded while the product's managed lane bounds the same loop at three rounds. The workflow never distinguished the first round, which assesses the whole delivered diff, from later rounds, which should verify closure of prior findings and review the delta, so every round was a whole-candidate search that always found something new on a surface every repair enlarged. And the workflow never said which findings block, so a marginal surviving mutant weighed the same as a broken guarantee, even though the shipped charters already carry a severity vocabulary where P2 and P3 are deferrable.

This plan closes the three gaps in workflow text only: a declared bound with the product's default of three rounds, verification rounds after the first, and blocking gated on P0 and P1. No reducer, schema, or kernel changes. The harness stops carrying thinner copies of two shipped charters and references the shipped set by identity, as Athena already does, with its two persona-reading scripts resolving charters from the installed archive; Athena's overlay drops its "no numeric iteration cap" sentence for the declared bound. The proof is the stopped ticket, re-run from its preserved pre-review candidate.

Operator decisions that bound this plan (2026-09-02): stop the non-convergent run and redesign the workflow before any run-surface work resumes; the default bound is three, the kernel's constant; in a verification round only a P0 outside the delta blocks; the harness moves to the shipped charters by identity. The kernel's constant is never passed to the reducer: the managed lane's own loop counts only findings-driven rounds against it. This plan passes three as the reducer's `max_rounds`, which counts every round obtained, so "three" here means three rounds in total, one findings-carrying round fewer than the managed lane admits (see R1 and Open Questions).

## Problem Frame

The V26-1548 session (harness projects directory, session `8f35f38e…`) is the evidence:

1. **No bound.** `review-work` requires "a positive round bound" as an input and the reducer's `max_rounds` enforces it with `review loop exceeded` and `review loop exhausted` blockers. `execute-work` and the kernel's Review phase never tell the executor to declare one. The executor declared none and ran seventeen rounds. The harness's managed lane, which executors do not drive, bounds the same loop with `REVIEW_ROUND_BOUND = 3` and records `review.loop-bound-reached`. Athena's overlay states "There is no numeric iteration cap".
2. **Every round a fresh campaign.** Rounds 1 to 6 raised twenty-nine surviving mutations. From round 7 the executor's lens brief read "mount a fresh mutation campaign against the production code and find a mutation that survives; go where earlier rounds have not concentrated". Per round the lens ran 24 to 103 mutations and returned between zero and ten survivors, a different set each time. The executor also accumulated its own thirty-item list of "standing rulings" (equivalent and unreachable mutants) to keep the lens from re-reporting, which is adjudication the workflow gave to no one. The candidate grew from 17 files and 4,249 added lines at the first gate-green commit (`ac421ae`, tree `a7d7fe2`) to 18 files and 8,574 added lines (`6daa1f5`), most of it tests.
3. **Every finding blocked.** The shipped `persona.testing-policy` charter carries P0 to P3 with "P2 and P3 are deferrable"; the harness's repository-owned copy at `delivery/personas/testing-policy.md` omits the severity vocabulary, the deferral rule, and the declined-findings rule, and the executor's result shape for the lens carried `survived: true` and no severity. Athena's overlay already has both severity gating (P0 and P1 in scope by definition, P2 and P3 deferrable with a filed follow-up) and churn control ("a finding first raised in a later round against code already approved in an earlier round is not automatically blocking"), so the rules exist; they exist only in one adopter's overlay.

The correctness lens re-ran ten times on an unchanged verdict because the binding rule requires every lens on every new candidate. That rule is right and stays; what it needs is a cheap task for a lens whose prior verdict was aligned.

## Requirements

- R1. The round bound belongs to the delivery, not to one review loop: it is declared before the first round, and every round obtained for the delivery counts against it, across every loop the delivery opens. A round in which every lens returned `aligned`, a round in which a lens failed, and a round obtained after an aligned round because a repair, rebase, or fix changed the candidate all count the same as a round with findings. The workflow default is three rounds, the operator's figure; it is passed as the reducer's `max_rounds`, with the rounds already obtained for the delivery supplied to every reduction, and the executor obtains no round beyond it. A default of three therefore admits at most two rounds that carry findings plus the round that verifies their closure. A repository may declare a different bound only in its root `AGENTS.md` or a file it delegates to, quoted by the executor when it states the bound in effect, and only a bound of three or fewer; a larger bound is an operator decision recorded on the ticket before the first round, never an executor's. The executor may not declare a bound for itself, and may not declare a second bound for the same delivery. The lens set is declared with the bound, before the first round, and held unchanged for the delivery: every declared lens is selected in every round, no lens is added after the first round, and dropping a lens is not a disposition of anything it filed. Because the set is fixed from round 1, each lens's carry-forward runs from round 1 and a gap in it is always a defect. A lens whose acquisition failed in the latest round makes the reducer's outcome `blocked` at once, so a failed round is not a way past a lens; before the bound the executor obtains the next round within the remaining bound, since the reducer judges the latest round.
- R2. Reaching the bound is terminal for the executor in each of its three forms. With active dissent, the review outcome is `blocked`, the delivery result is `partial` with a typed blocker `review.loop-bound-reached` naming the open findings, and the executor runs no further round. Without dissent but with a repair, rebase, or fix still required that would change the candidate, no round remains to bind the new candidate, so the delivery stops as `partial` with the same blocker naming the unreviewed change, and the executor neither obtains another round nor reports `complete` on an unreviewed candidate. With the latest round blocked by a failed acquisition and no round left, the delivery stops as `partial` with the same blocker naming the failed lens. The operator decides what happens next.
- R3. Round kind is per lens. A lens's assessment round is its first result-carrying round for the delivery, round 1 unless its round-1 acquisition failed, and reviews the whole delivered diff; every later result-carrying round of that lens is a verification round, and its delta runs from the candidate of that lens's most recent result-carrying entry, so a round in which the lens's acquisition failed is spanned rather than skipped. In a verification round every selected lens re-runs on the new candidate, as the binding rule requires, but each lens first re-checks every carried finding of its own, deferrals included, and reports each closed, open, raised with its new severity, withdrawn with its reason, or still deferred, then reviews only the delta between the previous round's candidate and this one. The acquisition envelope carries one `candidateRef` per round, so a verification round's `contextRefs` carry, for each lens, that lens's own retained entry from every prior round of the delivery in which it was selected, contiguous over rounds 1 through N-1, each as `obtain-review` returned it, binding tuple and result together where the state is `obtained`, and binding tuple with its result-less state where the acquisition failed, that entry accounting for the round while the delta spans it, so that every prior report arrives bound to its round and its `candidateRef`; plus, for each of the two `candidateRef` values the delta spans, that reference's declared resolution as a pair of the exact `candidateRef` text (its `opaque` member) and the revision it resolves to; and nothing else, nothing narrower. The previous round's `candidateRef` is read from the previous entry, never supplied beside it. The lens re-derives its carried set from those entries rather than from any list the caller hands it, and derives the delta itself from that entry's `candidateRef` and the current one. A `candidateRef` is opaque and is never parsed, so the adopter's repository instructions declare how a `candidateRef` resolves to a revision: in the harness and in Athena it is the candidate tree the harness review context records. The lens matches each pair's `candidateRef` text by equality, never by parsing, against its most recent entry and the current binding. The two pairs are supplied material, not a narrowing of scope; a resolution missing, unpaired, or paired with a reference that is neither of those two is the defect described next. A lens cannot return `failed`; the envelope's `failed` state is the caller's record of an acquisition failure, carries no result, and is never how a lens speaks. A lens that finds its carry-forward defective, because the supplied entries are not exactly its own entries for every prior round of the delivery, a round accounted for by neither an obtained entry nor a result-less one included, because a resolution is missing, unpaired, or paired with a reference that is neither of the two the delta spans, or because any narrower scope is supplied, returns `changes-requested` with a P0 finding naming the defect, which blocks and which only that lens can close. A new finding outside the delta blocks only at P0; a new P1 outside the delta is recorded as a deferral with a follow-up.
- R4. Only P0 and P1 findings inside a round's scope are actionable. A lens records every non-blocking finding in its evidence as a deferral, not in `findings`: P2 and P3 in any round, and a P1 first filed outside the delta in a verification round. A carried finding's filed severity and in-scope status are a floor: on re-check the filing lens may raise a carried finding's severity, never lower it, and a raised finding is treated as filed at the new severity from that round, so a carried deferral raised to P0 blocks under the out-of-delta rule; a carried P0 or P1 is discharged only by the filing lens's closure report or by that lens's withdrawal with its reason, never by a later deferral; only a finding the lens recorded as a deferral when it filed it is discharged by that deferral. A deferral stays in the carried set for re-checking, since a repair can raise its severity, but while it stays deferred it does not bar alignment. A lens returns `aligned` only when every carried finding of its own is discharged and it has no actionable finding open. Each deferral gets the follow-up handoff the kernel's Compound phase already names (a tracked follow-up when a tracker is configured, otherwise a recorded handoff).
- R5. The executor records a disposition for every finding a lens filed or deferred, and every disposition cites the lens: closed only by citing the closure report of the lens that filed the finding, in a named later round; deferred only where that lens itself recorded the deferral, with the follow-up; declined only when the lens itself withdrew the finding in a later round, with the lens's stated reason; or open, named in the `review.loop-bound-reached` blocker when the bound is reached. The executor may not close, defer, or decline a finding on its own judgement. The round's evidence carries the dispositions; the ticket carries the bound in effect, the round count, and the dispositions at handoff.
- R6. No reducer, result-shape, workflow-graph, or kernel change. `review-lens-result/1`, `review-acquisition-envelope/1`, `workflow-graph/1`, and the reducer's `ReviewRequest` are unchanged; every qualification scenario id and script is unchanged.
- R7. The harness policy references `persona.outcome-correctness` and `persona.testing-policy` by identity, the shipped set resolving the digest at compile, and carries no repository-owned charter copies; the two scripts that read charters resolve them from the installed archive, with bytes verified against the compiled snapshot's digests, and fail closed.
- R8. Athena's delivery overlay declares one bound for the whole delivery, three rounds counted across its two review stages, the pre-validation checkpoint's repeat step and the post-validation loop, each stage stating that its rounds count against that one total and neither carrying a cap of its own, and no longer states that there is no iteration cap; its churn-control and severity rules stay.

## Key Technical Decisions

- D1. **The bound is a workflow default, not a policy member, and counts every round of the delivery.** The kernel's policy grammar is closed; adding a `reviewRoundBound` member would be a kernel change for a number the workflow can carry. The default is three rounds in total for the delivery, passed unchanged as `max_rounds` with every round already obtained supplied to each reduction; a second loop opened after alignment continues the same count rather than starting a fresh one, which is what turned V26-1082's one round into "one plus a second loop" without any bound noticing. A repository instruction file may declare three or fewer, quoted by the executor when it states the bound; more is an operator decision on the ticket. The executor never chooses its own bound and never declares a second.
- D2. **The bound is terminal for the executor whether or not dissent is open.** The reducer already returns `blocked` at the bound with dissent; the workflow text makes the consequence explicit: `partial` with `review.loop-bound-reached`, the same code the kernel facade uses, and no further round. The case the reducer cannot see, alignment followed by a required candidate change with no round left, gets the same outcome, because the alternative is either an unreviewed candidate reported `complete` or a fresh bound. An executor that runs another round after either has left the workflow.
- D3. **Assessment round, then verification rounds, by ordinal, with a carry-forward the lens can check.** Round 1 reviews the whole delivered diff. Every later round keeps the binding rule (every lens, new candidate) but changes each lens's task: re-check of every one of its own carried findings, deferrals included, then the delta only. This is the churn-control rule Athena's overlay already states, made the product's rule. The acquisition envelope is closed and carries a single `candidateRef`, so under R6 the prior material travels as `contextRefs`, which are already caller-retained: the lens's own entries from every prior round's retained envelope, binding tuple and result together, with the previous round's `candidateRef` read from the previous entry. Giving the lens its own bound entries, not a caller-picked list of findings, is what makes an omitted round and a substituted reference detectable, since the entries must be contiguous over rounds 1 through N-1 with a failed acquisition present as its result-less entry, and each resolution pair must name by exact text a reference the lens already holds; whether a supplied resolution is the true one for its reference rests on the retained round evidence and the disposition record, which the operator audits, not on the lens, and the lens speaks about a defect through a P0 finding because the result shape admits nothing else; without them the carry-forward would rest on executor good faith, which is the discretion V26-1548's "standing rulings" exercised.
- D4. **Only P0 and P1 block; a lens self-classifies; deferrals are one bucket.** The charters already define the vocabulary and say P2 and P3 are deferrable. The workflow tells the lens where a non-blocking finding goes (evidence, as a deferral with a follow-up) and when it returns `aligned` (all its prior findings closed, nothing actionable open). The reducer sees deferrals as evidence, not findings, so it is unchanged. In a verification round a new finding outside the delta blocks only at P0 (operator decision); a P1 there is deferred, not dropped.
- D5. **Dispositions are the executor's record, not a new artifact, and every one cites the lens.** Closed (citing the filing lens's closure report in a named round), deferred (where the lens recorded the deferral), or declined (lens-withdrawn only), carried in the round's evidence and on the ticket at handoff. The executor adjudicates nothing; V26-1548's "standing rulings" were adjudication through `closed` as much as through `declined`, and both routes are shut. This is what the run surface's `review.round.closed` findings-by-severity will read from later; nothing new is persisted by `review-work`.
- D6. **The harness uses the shipped charters by identity, resolved from the installed archive.** Its two repository-owned copies are thinner than the shipped set (no severity vocabulary) and exist only because they predate it. Identity references are what the shipped set was built for and what Athena already does. The projection check supplies the composition-origin persona set through `projectShippedPersonas` over a directory-backed reader on `.agent-skills/current`, which is the production caller that function has lacked. The evidence emitter's reviewer set stays the two activated lenses of the compiled snapshot, not the whole shipped set; each charter is read from the installed archive and its bytes verified against the snapshot's digest, failing closed on a mismatch or a missing file. Removing the copies drops the two digests from the policy; the compiled snapshot keeps a digest per lens, now resolved at compile from the shipped charter rather than pinned, and the edit is a reviewed edit to the protected `.agents/policy/` tree, performable because the installation is in shadow mode with no grant hook active.
- D7. **Athena's overlay keeps its two-stage shape.** The overlay declares one bound for the delivery, and the pre-validation checkpoint's repeat step and the post-validation loop each say that their rounds count against that one total; the sentence claiming no cap goes. Nothing else in the overlay changes.
- D8. **The proof starts before the failure, and reads records, not transcripts.** The re-run starts from the pre-review candidate `ac421ae`, not from the fully repaired `6daa1f5`, so the review sees the candidate the seventeen rounds saw. Its finish line is read from the ticket, the delivery record, and the round evidence; the transcript is observability, mined afterwards for field data, and never a sensor or a finish-line condition.

## Open Questions

Resolved by the operator on 2026-09-02: the default bound is three; an out-of-delta P0 in a verification round blocks and nothing below P0 outside the delta does; the harness moves to the shipped charters by identity. Five refinements were settled by plan review rather than by the operator and are recorded here for the operator's veto: "three" is three rounds in total as the reducer counts them, for the whole delivery rather than per loop, which is stricter than the managed lane's constant, that lane admitting three findings-driven rounds plus the aligning round (R1); alignment followed by a candidate change with no round left ends `partial` rather than in a fresh bound (R2); the proof unit starts from the pre-review candidate (D8); a lens's own recorded deferral discharges a carried finding for alignment while staying re-checked each round (R4); and the truth of a supplied `candidateRef` resolution rests on the retained round evidence the operator audits, the lens checking only that each pair names a reference it holds (R3, D3).

Ruled by the operator on 2026-09-02, after the bounded workflow proved itself on the V26-1548 re-run: the default bound is four rather than three, and a required candidate change after alignment obtains one grace verification round, at most once per delivery, rather than ending `partial` at once — superseding the first two refinements above — while the remaining three (D8, R4, and R3 with D3) are accepted as written; V26-1575 carries both rulings into the workflow text and the release it cuts, and V26-1576 and V26-1577 install that release and restate the bound and the grace round in the harness's and Athena's overlays.

## High-Level Technical Design

**`review-work`.** Inputs: the round bound is "declared by the caller before the first round and belongs to the delivery: every round obtained for the delivery counts against it, across every loop, and every round already obtained is supplied to each reduction; the workflow default is three rounds and a repository instruction file may declare a smaller bound; a larger bound is an operator decision recorded before the first round". Convergence: an actionable finding is one the lens filed at P0 or P1 inside the round's scope; a deferral recorded in evidence is not a finding; a lens is `aligned` only when every carried finding of its own is discharged, by that lens's closure report, by that lens's withdrawal with its reason, or, for a finding it filed as a deferral, by that deferral, and it has no actionable finding open. `blocked` at the bound is terminal: the caller records a typed blocker `review.loop-bound-reached` naming the open findings and does not run another round.

**`obtain-review`.** A new "Round kinds" section: round kind is per lens; a lens's first result-carrying round is its assessment round over the whole delivered diff, and every later result-carrying round of that lens is a verification round, including a round obtained after alignment because the candidate changed, with the delta running from the lens's most recent result-carrying entry so a failed acquisition is spanned. ## Alternative Approaches Considered

- **A policy member for the bound.** Rejected: the policy grammar is closed and the number belongs to the workflow; the operator's figure of three is the default.
- **Count only rounds that carry findings.** Rejected: the reducer counts every round supplied, and an executor-adjudicated count of "findings-driven" rounds is executor discretion over the bound, the thing this plan removes.
- **Cap mutations per round instead of rounds.** Rejected: a mutation budget bounds each round's cost but not the number of rounds; round bound plus verification rounds bounds both.
- **Scope the testing lens to the diff of the round only, no verification of prior findings.** Rejected: a repair can reopen a prior finding; closure must be checked on the new candidate.
- **Upgrade the harness's charter copies to the shipped text.** Rejected in favour of identity references: same policy re-pin, more bytes to keep in step.
- **Re-run the proof from the fully repaired candidate.** Rejected: it starts downstream of the failure and can satisfy the finish line in one round without exercising a verification round.

## Risk Analysis & Mitigation

- **A real defect deferred at P2, or a real P1 outside the delta deferred in a verification round.** Accepted by the operator's decision: the charters' vocabulary ties severity to the delivery's central claim; every deferral carries a follow-up; the operator sees dispositions on the ticket and can reopen any deferral before merge.
